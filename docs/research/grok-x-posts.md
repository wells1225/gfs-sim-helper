# The Sims 4 crashes, mods, and CurseForge: posts on X

Searched X on 24 Nov 2026. Window: about 24 Nov 2025 through 24 Nov 2026. Keyword search, semantic search, and thread reads. Every item below is a post that came back in that search. Nothing here is filled in from memory.

**Official** means the account is @TheSims, @TheSimsDirect (its bio says it posts status for @TheSims), or @CurseForge. Everything else is a player or a mod creator. Creator posts are labeled **creator**. Fan news (@TheSimCommunity, @gamuters, @VelvettBYT) is labeled **fan news**, not EA.

Post links are `https://x.com/<handle>/status/<id>`. Dates are the post timestamps in GMT.

## What repeats

Players talk about three different failures and often mix them up.

1. **The March 2026 patch stopped package files from loading**, then a follow-up left some people on a black screen or "Failed to launch." @TheSimsDirect said the team found the cause and later shipped a fix. That one is a game bug that hit people who use custom content, not one broken mod.
2. **After ordinary patches, script mods go stale.** The names that come up are UI Cheats Extension, MC Command Center, and "script mods" as a class. Clothes and furniture are treated as safer. Several creators post a same-day "still compatible" note. Several players say they still have to hunt.
3. **The game also crashes with mods removed.** Memory, a freeze on the title screen (including on a Mac with no mods), and Sims freezing in live mode show up in official patch notes and in player posts. A helper that only blames the Mods folder will miss those.

No single mod dominates the crash posts in this window. The closest to a repeated name is UI Cheats Extension, and even that is "update it after a patch," not "this file crashes the game."

### What EA tells creators to expect

From the @TheSimsDirect posts above, in their words: before a significant base-game update, back up saves and prepare mods. Pre-patch TDESC files are an early look. "Mod updates still require patch-day tuning & scripts." Some updates are "minimal impact" or server-side and leave mods alone. When custom content is involved in a launch failure, they send people to the best-practices article and ask which mods were installed, including ones already removed.
